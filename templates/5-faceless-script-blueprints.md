## Global Production Rules (Apply to All 5 Blueprints)

- **Hook inside `0:00–0:30`**: Open with a physical object, clear constraint, or visual contradiction. Skip channel intros and "welcome back" greetings.
- **Visual swap every 8–12 seconds**: Pair every narration beat with a distinct visual cue (`[B-ROLL]`, `[DIAGRAM]`, `[ARCHIVE]`, or `[ON-SCREEN CARD]`). Do not leave static stock footage running for 30+ seconds.
- **Original narration only**: Reference captions establish pacing and verifiable facts; rewrite every spoken line in your own voice and drop host jokes or sponsor reads.
- **On-screen attribution**: Label archives, museum collections, dates, and primary records in the lower-third. If a claim is not backed by a source, cut it or mark it clearly as a hypothesis.

## Blueprint 1: Documentary / Explainer

Best suited for: Ancient Civilizations, Museum Object Stories, Forgotten Inventions, Architecture Styles Explained, Rail & Transit Journeys.

```markdown
[TITLE]: [Specific Object/Event]: How [Concrete Detail] Solved/Changed [Outcome]

[0:00–0:30 COLD OPEN HOOK]
- [B-ROLL]: Close-up archival image or museum object plate with date stamp.
- [VO]: Open on one concrete artifact or turning point. State the central puzzle in one spoken sentence without background fluff.

[0:30–1:30 SCOPE & DEFINITIONS]
- [DIAGRAM]: Simple timeline or map pinpointing location and year.
- [VO]: Define 1–2 plain-English terms the viewer needs. State clearly what this episode covers and what it leaves out.

[1:30–3:00 EVIDENCE BEAT 1 — THE BOTTLENECK]
- [ARCHIVE]: Primary document, blueprint, or historical photograph (swap angle every 8–12s).
- [VO]: Explain why existing methods failed before the breakthrough. Anchor on one verifiable date or physical constraint.

[3:00–4:30 EVIDENCE BEAT 2 — THE MECHANISM / TURNING POINT]
- [DIAGRAM]: Labeled breakdown showing how the solution worked step by step.
- [VO]: Walk through the causal chain ("because X happened, Y became possible"). Keep sentences under 20 words for clean TTS pacing.

[4:30–5:45 EVIDENCE BEAT 3 — CONTRAST OR SECOND CASE]
- [B-ROLL]: Side-by-side comparison card contrasting a rival approach or common misconception.
- [VO]: Show a second documented case so the viewer sees the broader pattern, not an isolated trivia fact.

[5:45–6:30 RECAP CARD & CLOSE]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: 3-bullet summary + source credits (archive/museum/paper).
- [VO]: Summarize the three takeaways in 20 seconds and point to one related episode.
```

## Blueprint 2: Business Case Study

Best suited for: Small Business Case Studies, Supply Chain Journeys, Brand Origin Stories, Quiet Luxury & Watch Culture, Personal Finance Basics.

```markdown
[TITLE]: How [Company/Model] Turned [Specific Constraint] Into [Operational Edge]

[0:00–0:25 CONSTRAINT HOOK]
- [B-ROLL]: Product close-up, flat-pack box, store floor plan, or receipt breakdown.
- [VO]: Lead with one counter-intuitive business trade-off (e.g., asking buyers to assemble furniture, shrinking floor space, or cutting SKU count).

[0:25–1:45 BACKSTORY & MARKET REALITY]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: Clean 3-row table showing the baseline cost, space, or shipping constraint.
- [VO]: Establish the exact bottleneck the founder or operator faced. Cite public filings, interviews, or documented store specs.

[1:45–3:30 UNIT ECONOMICS BREAKDOWN]
- [DIAGRAM]: Visual cost stack or flow chart (input -> operational step -> margin impact).
- [VO]: Walk through 2–3 concrete levers (square footage, shipping density, private-label share, repeat frequency). Avoid buzzwords.

[3:30–5:00 FAILURE MODE OR RIVAL COMPARISON]
- [B-ROLL]: Contrast with a competitor or a phase where the tactic broke down.
- [VO]: Explain where this tactic fails or hits diminishing returns so the analysis stays honest and balanced.

[5:00–6:00 TRANSFERABLE RULE]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: One reusable checklist for small operators or creators.
- [VO]: Distill one rule viewers can apply to their own project or budget, and link to a companion case study.
```

## Blueprint 3: Historical Mystery (Non-Gore)

Best suited for: Unsolved Mysteries (Non-Gore), Shipwrecks & Maritime Mysteries, Museum Object Stories, Forgotten Inventions.

```markdown
[TITLE]: [Case/Event]: What the Physical Evidence Proves (and What Stays Open)

[0:00–0:40 EVIDENCE LOG HOOK]
- [ARCHIVE]: Map coordinates, dated logbook scan, or site diagram.
- [VO]: State two verified facts from the primary record that appear to contradict each other. Promise a strict split between documented facts and theories.

[0:40–1:45 GROUND RULES & TIMELINE]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: Two-column graphic labeled "Verified Record" vs "Later Claim".
- [VO]: Set the exact date, place, and actors. Tell the viewer upfront how you will label speculation on screen.

[1:45–3:45 VERIFIED EVIDENCE SEQUENCE]
- [B-ROLL]: Sequential timeline cards (Day 1 -> Day 2 -> Recovery) paired with public maps or weather records.
- [VO]: Walk strictly through what investigators, museums, or maritime logs recorded—nothing added from rumors.

[3:45–5:30 COMPETING HYPOTHESES TESTED AGAINST EVIDENCE]
- [DIAGRAM]: Matrix testing Theory A vs Theory B against 3 physical clues (checkmarks and gaps).
- [VO]: Show what each theory explains well and which specific clue it fails to account for.

[5:30–6:30 OPEN STATUS & SOURCE CARD]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: Current consensus status + links/titles of cited archives.
- [VO]: End honestly on what remains unproven without sensational claims or gore.
```

## Blueprint 4: Science & Engineering How-It-Works

Best suited for: Electricity & Power Grids, Dams & Reservoirs, Tunnels & Underground Engineering, Cooking Science (No Faces), Cybersecurity for Everyday Users.

```markdown
[TITLE]: How [System/Machine] Works Without Failing Under [Real-World Stress]

[0:00–0:30 VISUAL PUZZLE HOOK]
- [B-ROLL]: Footage of the real physical phenomenon (washboard road, power substation, boiling emulsion, tunnel boring head).
- [VO]: Ask one concrete "why doesn't this break?" or "why does this shape form?" question that viewers can see on screen.

[0:30–1:30 PLAIN-ENGLISH DEFINITION]
- [DIAGRAM]: Minimalist 3-part block diagram labeling inputs, core component, and output.
- [VO]: Name the two core variables in everyday language before introducing any technical term.

[1:30–4:15 CAUSE-AND-EFFECT CHAIN (3 BEATS)]
- [DIAGRAM]: Animated arrows or step-by-step cross-section (change visual state every 8–12s).
  - Beat A (Input): What enters the system and why scale matters.
  - Beat B (Transformation): Where force, heat, current, or pressure shifts state.
  - Beat C (Control Loop): What keeps the system balanced when load spikes.
- [VO]: Keep each beat tied to what is visually highlighted on the diagram.

[4:15–5:45 DESIGN COMPARISON OR EDGE CASE]
- [DIAGRAM]: Side-by-side comparison (e.g., Arch Dam vs Gravity Dam; TBM vs Immersed Tube).
- [VO]: Show why engineers pick Option A in one terrain and Option B in another.

[5:45–6:30 SUMMARY CARD]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: 3-step mechanism summary + primary engineering references.
- [VO]: Recap the causal rule in one clean sentence.
```

## Blueprint 5: Myth-Flip

Best suited for: Personal Finance Basics, Career Skills for Remote Workers, Cybersecurity for Everyday Users, Roads & Pavement, Tides & Ocean Currents.

```markdown
[TITLE]: Myth Flip: Why [Popular Advice/Assumption] Fails — and What Actually Works

[0:00–0:30 COMMON ASSUMPTION HOOK]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: Bold quote card displaying the common belief in plain text.
- [VO]: State the assumption viewers already believe, then immediately introduce one everyday counter-example that breaks it.

[0:30–1:45 WHY THE MYTH SOUNDS TRUE]
- [B-ROLL]: Relatable everyday scene illustrating where the intuition comes from.
- [VO]: Respect the viewer's intelligence—explain why the myth feels obvious on the surface before dismantling it.

[1:45–4:15 THE REAL MECHANISM (2–3 CONCRETE STEPS)]
- [DIAGRAM]: Split-screen "Myth Path" vs "Actual Mechanism" showing exact steps or numbers.
- [VO]: Replace the vague slogan with a concrete system (e.g., sinking-fund buckets vs waiting for a raise; MFA + password manager vs "complex password memorization").

[4:15–5:15 EDGE CASES & EXCEPTIONS]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: "When this rule does NOT apply" callout box.
- [VO]: Name one clear boundary condition so the video feels grounded rather than preachy.

[5:15–6:00 VIEWER SELF-CHECKLIST]
- [ON-SCREEN CARD]: 3-item check viewers can run in 5 minutes after watching.
- [VO]: Close with one practical check and point to the next video in the series.
```
